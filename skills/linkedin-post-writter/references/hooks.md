# Hook Patterns

The first 2-3 lines are all a reader sees before "See more". They do one job:
earn the click. A hook that promises more than the post delivers costs you more
than a weak hook does.

## The Nine Patterns

### 1. Honest admission

Opens by conceding something. Works because almost nobody leads with being
wrong.

- "I've been wrong about remote work."
- "I turned down the best offer of my career. It was a mistake."
- "I spent two years building the wrong thing."
- "I don't actually know how to hire well. Here's what I've narrowed down."

**Fails when:** the admission is a humblebrag ("My biggest weakness is caring
too much").

### 2. Specific observation

A pattern you've seen, stated with enough specificity to prove you've actually
seen it.

- "I've noticed something in every founder who scaled past $10M."
- "Every failed migration I've worked on failed for the same reason."
- "The engineers who get promoted fastest do one unglamorous thing."

**Fails when:** the specificity is fake. "Every single one of the 47 CEOs I've
coached" reads as invented.

### 3. Direct challenge

States that a widely held belief is wrong. Requires you to actually deliver a
better belief.

- "Most career advice optimizes for the wrong thing."
- "Culture fit is the most expensive hiring mistake there is."
- "Your roadmap is not a strategy."

**Fails when:** you're contrarian for the sake of it and the post walks it back
to conventional wisdom.

### 4. Unexpected angle

Subverts what the reader expects the sentence to end with.

- "The best hire I made had the worst resume."
- "Our most profitable quarter came from the customer we almost fired."
- "The meeting I dreaded most became the one I protect."

**Fails when:** the subversion isn't real and the post is a normal story.

### 5. Simple truth

Names something people feel but don't say. No cleverness.

- "Nobody talks about how lonely leadership is."
- "Most people quit managers, not jobs. You already know that. Here's the part
  nobody says."
- "Getting good at your job doesn't make you less afraid of it."

**Fails when:** it's a platitude. "Hard work pays off" is not a truth, it's a
poster.

### 6. Concrete number in context

A number that means something, not a number that sounds impressive.

- "We cut deploy time from 40 minutes to 4. The 40 was never the problem."
- "Three of my last five hires came from the same rejected-candidate pile."

**Fails when:** it's a revenue flex. "$1.2M in 90 days" reads as an ad.

### 7. The question you actually can't answer

Genuine open question you're thinking through in public.

- "How do you tell a strong performer their scope is shrinking?"
- "When does 'we'll fix it later' become a lie?"

**Fails when:** it's rhetorical and you clearly have the answer teed up.

### 8. In-progress, not concluded

Shares a decision before the outcome is known.

- "I'm about to do something my last three managers would have told me not to."
- "We're rewriting the service everyone told us to leave alone. Day 4."

**Fails when:** you never follow up.

### 9. The correction

Revisits something you said publicly before.

- "Two years ago I posted that hiring seniors always beats growing juniors. I
  want to take part of that back."

**Fails when:** you have no such prior post.

## Hooks to Retire

| Retired hook | Why |
|---|---|
| "This one thing made me $X" | Reads as a scam ad |
| "The CEO pulled me aside and said..." | Overused curiosity bait |
| "I'm excited to announce..." | Corporate and skippable |
| "[Number] words that changed my life" | Too formulaic |
| "Let that sink in." | Empty emphasis |
| "Unpopular opinion:" | The opinion that follows is usually popular |
| "I got rejected 100 times. Then..." | The redemption arc is exhausted |
| "Hot take 🔥" | Signals the take is not hot |
| "Read that again." | Instructs the reader to be impressed |
| Airport / Uber driver / barista wisdom | Recycled viral format, assumed fake |

## Testing a Hook

Four checks before you commit to one:

1. **Truth test** — is every word literally true? Hooks are where posts start
   lying.
2. **Stranger test** — would you click this from someone you've never heard of?
3. **Delivery test** — does the post pay off the specific promise in line 1?
4. **Cut test** — delete the first sentence. Is the post better? Often the real
   hook was line 2.

## Rewriting Weak Hooks

| Weak | Why | Stronger |
|---|---|---|
| "Here are 5 tips for better 1:1s." | No reason to care | "My 1:1s were useless for a year and my reports were too polite to say so." |
| "Leadership is hard." | Platitude | "Nobody warns you that the hardest part of leading is having no one to ask." |
| "Excited to share we raised our Series A!" | Announcement, not a post | "We raised a Series A. The pitch that worked was the fourth one, and it was the one I hated." |
| "AI is changing everything." | No claim | "Most AI pilots I've reviewed fail before the model is ever involved." |
